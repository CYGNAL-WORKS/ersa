# The composer

How the composer and the session controls look, token by token. `FLOW-SEND-MESSAGE` says how the composer behaves, `FLOW-NEW-SESSION` how the session controls choose where and how a session runs, and `LAY-SHELL` where both sit; `DESIGN.md` holds the tokens and the rules every surface follows. Read this before changing the composer or the session controls.

The owner reviewed this design as mockups on the design canvas: the Composer board draws the composer's states, and the Place picker board draws the project list, the no-projects state and the trust question, and a workspace list that the Worktree checkbox has replaced. Build what this page and the briefs describe; where a board, the built components or the gallery differ from this page, this page wins. The chips built before this page have an edge; the ticket that restyles the session controls changes them, and the gallery follows. The Composer board also draws a microphone for dictation, which is not specified (FLOW-SEND-MESSAGE, Open questions): leave it out.

## The composer

The composer is one column, `size-measure` wide at most and centered, holding from top to bottom: the status line, the box and the session controls, `space-xs` apart. In a session it sits on the transcript's canvas with no line between them: the transcript's last `space-xxxl` fades out behind it.

- **The box** holds what the user writes: the attach button on the leading edge, the words, then send. It is `material-composer` with a `line` edge at `hairline`, `radius-md`, padded `space-sm`, its parts `space-sm` apart and aligned to the bottom, so the buttons stay by the last line as the words grow.
- **The words** are `prose` in `ink`, padded `space-xs` by `space-sm`, with no edge of their own; the placeholder is `ink-faint`. The field is one line tall, on the new-session screen as in a session, and grows with the words.
- **Attach** is a quiet icon button, `size-control-lg` square, `radius-md`, with a plus icon at `size-icon` in `ink-muted`. It is a control without an edge (`DESIGN.md`, Pointer and press): under the pointer it takes `material-hover` and reads `ink`, pressed or while its menu is open it takes `material-pressed` and reads `ink`, and with keyboard focus its edge turns `ink`.
- **Send** is the primary button made square: `size-control-lg`, `radius-md`, `material-primary`, with an up arrow at `size-icon` in `accent-ink`. Under the pointer `material-hover` lies over its fill, and pressed `material-pressed`; with keyboard focus it takes an `ink` edge. With nothing to send it has no fill, a `line` edge and an `ink-faint` arrow, and does not change under the pointer.
- **Stop** takes send's place from the moment the user sends until the turn ends: the same square on `material-tile` with a `line` edge, holding a filled square at `size-icon-sm` in `ink`. It is a control with an edge (`DESIGN.md`, Pointer and press), and with keyboard focus its edge turns `ink`. Once chosen, stop is taken until the turn it stops has ended: the same square with its icon in `ink-faint`, unchanged under the pointer, named "Stopping…", keeping keyboard focus and taking no second choice (FLOW-SEND-MESSAGE-R21). It takes stop again when that turn ends, whatever was sent meanwhile, or at once when the stop only canceled a message on its way. When stop turns back into send, keyboard focus stays on the button. While the agent is working, Enter still sends what the box holds (FLOW-SEND-MESSAGE-R18), though the button stays stop; before the turn has begun, what is written stays in the box (FLOW-SEND-MESSAGE-R02).

Their fills and edges change over `motion-quick` with `motion-ease-out`. Send becoming stop and back crossfades over `motion-quick`.

## Focus

- **Focused**, the box's edge is `accent` and it carries `glow-focal`, fading in over `motion-settle` with `motion-ease-out`: it is the screen's one focal glow.
- **Focused while a permission request is open**, the box's edge alone is `accent`; the glow stays on the request (`DESIGN.md`, Elevation & Depth).
- **Under the pointer**, unfocused, its edge is `ink-faint`.

## The status line

One line above the box, `size-control` high at least, showing one message at a time, first that Ersa can't reach the sessions, then, while the box is available, a system dialog for attachments that failed to open, then a send whose outcome Ersa can't tell, then a refused message, then a refused change to the session, then, on the new-session screen, detecting backends, then a host that couldn't list its backends or has none that can run, then a send on its way, then a message waiting in the backend; padded `space-sm` at the sides, in `ui-small` and `ink-muted`. It is empty, and keeps no height, while there is nothing to say. The composer keeps its box and session controls where they are: the status line opens upward, growing and fading in over `motion-settle` with `motion-ease-out`, and closes the same way.

| When | It says | Leading mark | The box |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ersa can't reach the sessions | that, and that it is trying again | none | unavailable |
| Ersa can't tell whether a message was sent | that, and that the transcript shows it if it arrived | a glyph at `size-glyph` in `caution` | as at rest, the message kept |
| A message was refused | the reason, in the user's terms | a glyph at `size-glyph` in `danger` | as at rest, the message kept |
| On the new-session screen, backends are being detected (FLOW-NEW-SESSION-R23) | that it is detecting the backends on this host | none | unavailable |
| On the new-session screen, the host couldn't list its backends (FLOW-NEW-SESSION-R11) | "Ersa couldn't detect the backends on this host." then the reason the host gave | none | unavailable |
| On the new-session screen, the host lists its backends and none can run | With every backend `missing`: "No backend is available on this host. Install a backend on it to start a session." With any backend in `error`: that backend by its label and what to do, such as "Claude Code isn't working on this host. Check that it's installed and signed in, then open New session again." (`backend.list` carries no reason for an error yet) | none | unavailable |
| The system's file or folder dialog failed to open (FLOW-SEND-MESSAGE-R23) | that Ersa couldn't open it, and to try again, until the user next chooses an attach item, attaches something or sends a message, or until a send Ersa can't tell the outcome of, a refused message or a refused change arrives after it (FLOW-SEND-MESSAGE-R24), which then shows by the order above and the dialog's line does not come back; it is the composer's own and not kept for its session, so it is gone when the user comes back from another session | a glyph at `size-glyph` in `danger` | as at rest |
| A message is on its way | how much is copied, as a share, that the session's folder is being put back (FLOW-CLEAN-UP-R05), or that the backend is starting | the ring at `size-ring`, its arc filling to the share over `motion-settle` | as at rest, stop offered |
| Changing the model, effort or permission mode was refused | the reason, in the user's terms, until the user sends or opens a chip in that session; it is kept for its session, so it is still there when the user comes back to it | a glyph at `size-glyph` in `danger` | as at rest |
| A message is waiting in the backend | One message: that it is waiting, and, in the window that sent it, its first line, cut with an ellipsis where the status line ends, else its file's name, else "Pasted image". Several: how many are waiting | none | as at rest, stop offered |

The line for a refused message, a refused change or a dialog that failed to open is announced to assistive technology as an alert. When the box becomes unavailable, keyboard focus returns to the words once it is available again. **Unavailable**, the box is `canvas`, the words and their placeholder read `ink-faint`, and attach and send read `ink-faint` with no fill and do not change under the pointer.

## The archived note

An archived session shows a note where the composer sits, at its measure: one row on `canvas` with a `line` edge at `hairline`, `radius-md`, padded `space-sm`. From the leading edge: an archive icon at `size-icon-sm` in `ink-muted`, the words in `ui` and `ink-muted`, then Resume, the primary button, at the trailing edge. While Core answers, Resume reads "Resuming…" and is unavailable. When Core says the session's folder is being put back meanwhile (FLOW-CLEAN-UP-R21), the note's words say so in place of the archived line, in the same style and as a status that assistive technology announces, as the status line says it of a message. The icon, the row and Resume keep their size and place, and the words crossfade over `motion-quick`, as send and stop do. The words stay with the resume when the user leaves the session and returns to it. A refused Resume says why above the note, where the status line sits, as a refused message does.

## Attachments

Attachments sit in the box above the words, across its full width, wrapping, `space-sm` apart, padded `space-xs` at the top and sides.

- **An attachment** is `size-control-lg` high on `material-tile`, with a `line` edge and `radius-md`, padded `space-sm` at the leading edge and `space-xs` at the trailing one, its parts `space-sm` apart, in `ui-small`. From the leading edge: its icon at `size-icon-sm` in `ink-muted` (an image, a file or a folder), its name in `ink` cut with an ellipsis, its detail in `ink-faint` (a size such as 84 KB, a line count such as 212 lines, or "folder"), and remove.
- **Remove** is an icon button `size-control-sm` square, `radius-sm`, with a cross at `size-icon-xs` in `ink-faint`; under the pointer it takes `material-hover` and reads `ink`, pressed `material-pressed`, and with keyboard focus its edge turns `ink`. Removing one moves keyboard focus to the next attachment's remove, else to the words.
- **One Ersa will copy** (`attachment.inspect`'s `copied`) says, after its detail, that it will be copied to the workspace's `.ersa` folder, in `ink-faint`.
- **One Ersa can't send** shows a glyph at `size-glyph` in `danger` in place of its icon, and its detail gives the reason in `danger`.
- **Dragging over the box**, its edge is `accent`, with no glow, until the drop or the drag leaves.
- An attachment fades in over `motion-quick` as it is added and out as it is removed.

## The attach menu

The attach menu is a chip's list (below) that opens upward from attach, `space-xs` above it. Its options lead with an icon at `size-icon-sm` in `ink-muted` in place of the option mark: Add files…, Add images…, Add a folder…. A note beneath them, in `ui-small` and `ink-faint`, padded `space-xs` by `space-sm`, says that attachments can also be pasted or dropped on the box.

## The session controls

One row beneath the box, `size-control` high at least, in `ui-small`, its chips `space-xs` apart. The where controls (the host chip, the project chip, then the Worktree checkbox) sit at the leading edge; the how chips (backend, model, effort, permission mode) and then the context ring sit at the trailing edge.
- **A chip** is a pill: `size-control` high, `radius-pill`, padded `space-sm` at the sides, with no fill and a `hairline` edge in no color at rest, its text `ink-muted`. A where chip leads with its icon at `size-icon-xs` in `ink-faint` (a monitor for the host, a folder for the project). A chip that opens a list ends with a down chevron at `size-icon-xs` in `ink-faint`.
- A chip is a control without an edge (`DESIGN.md`, Pointer and press). **Under the pointer** it takes `material-hover` and reads `ink`; **pressed**, `material-pressed`; **open**, `surface-raised` with a `line` edge, reading `ink`, whatever the pointer does; **with keyboard focus**, an `ink` edge. Unavailable, it reads `ink-faint` and does not change under the pointer.
- **The Worktree checkbox** (`FLOW-NEW-SESSION-R37`, `claude-desktop/new-session-row-2026-11-15.txt`) is a pill shaped like a chip, with no chevron: a box at `size-icon-xs`, Lucide's Square or, checked, SquareCheck, then the label "Worktree". The box is `ink-faint` unchecked and `accent` checked. Under the pointer, pressed and with keyboard focus it is as a chip is. Unavailable, while the first message is on its way and in a session, it reads `ink-faint`, box and all. Under the pointer and to assistive technology it says "The session works in an isolated copy of the repository."
- **In a session**, the chips are the same pills without the chevron or any change under the pointer: they show the session's values and do not open, except that a running session's model, effort and permission mode chips open as they do before it starts (FLOW-NEW-SESSION-R30). Those three keep a chevron's width on every session, the chevron fading in and out over `motion-quick` with `motion-ease-out` as the session starts and stops, so that nothing shifts. While a change is on its way the chip keeps its look and keyboard focus, reads its old value and does not open; it reads the new value once Core answers, or keeps the old one with the status line saying why, with focus on it either way.
- **The backend chip** is in the row whenever the host's backends are listed, with one backend as with several (FLOW-NEW-SESSION-R48): it reads the backend's label as the listing gives it, or "No backend" led by a glyph in `danger` when none can run, and its list gives each backend's version, or its status when it can't run.
- **Model** reads the backend's default as the listed model it stands for, with that model's row marked, and the default has no row of its own (FLOW-NEW-SESSION-R46).
- **Effort** shows its level alone, as the permission mode chip shows its mode; to assistive technology each chip still says which setting it is. With none chosen, and under a session that reports none, it reads the level the backend marks as its default, with that level marked in a list of the levels alone, and a session started so passes none to the backend (FLOW-NEW-SESSION-R47). Only where the backend marks no default, or the model doesn't take the one it marks, does it read Default, a value from the string catalog: then, before a session starts, Default is the first row of its list, chosen while no level is, and choosing it goes back to none; a running session's list holds only the levels. A model that takes no effort leaves the chip in its place, unavailable, reading None.
- **Permission mode** lists each mode with its description beneath it (FLOW-NEW-SESSION-R45; A chip's list, below).
- **Detection failed**, on the new-session screen when the host couldn't list its backends, the backend chip reads "Detection failed" led by a glyph in `danger`, and the status line says why.
- **While Ersa can't reach the sessions**, a running session's chips are unavailable, as the box is.
- **Detecting backends**, on the new-session screen, the how chips are replaced by one unavailable chip led by the context ring's track with a quarter arc in `ink-muted`, turning once per `motion-loop-ring` with `motion-linear`, saying that backends are being detected.
- **Under a session**, neither shows: the backend chip is in the row from the start, as the other pills are, and reads the session's backend by its id, with no glyph, until a listing names it (FLOW-NEW-SESSION-R49).
- **The context ring** is `size-ring`, a `line` track with an arc in `ink-muted` at the gauge's stroke, then the share used in tabular figures, `space-sm` apart. The arc turns `caution` in the ring's second band and `danger` in its third (`LAY-SHELL`, Context). It is never shorter than a twentieth of the ring, so a small share still shows, and a share under 1 percent reads "<1%". With no reading it is the track alone, with no figure: on a session it says that context isn't reported yet, and before a session starts, when there is nothing to report, that none is used yet; with a reading of nothing used it is the track alone and reads "0%". To assistive technology the ring says the share used and of what: the context, or the usage window it reads (`LAY-SHELL`, Context), by its label. In the session controls the ring is a tab stop, on a session and on the new-session screen alike, and with keyboard focus takes an `ink` edge as a chip does. Under the pointer or with keyboard focus it shows a tooltip above it that lists every reading, one to a line and never re-sorted: the context, then each window in the order reported. A line says the reading as the ring says it to assistive technology ("5-hour limit: 84 percent used"), then, at the trailing edge in `ink-muted`, when the window resets. Before a session starts the list is the context's one line. A reset a day or more away reads as Claude Desktop's list words it (`claude-desktop/usage-limits-2026-11-15.txt`): a weekday and time ("Resets Wed 2:00 PM"). The recording shows no nearer reset, so the rest is Ersa's own: under a day away it reads the time left, counted when the list opens, as "Resets in 1 hr 15 min", "Resets in 2 hr" or "Resets in 15 min", the minutes rounded up so the words never say a window has reset before it has. Once the time has passed the line says nothing of a reset, until the backend reports the window again. A window's share is the whole percent, rounded down, and never more than full. The nearest limit is the largest figure as shown, so the ring never reads less than a line of its own list: a context that reads 85 percent is nearer than a window at 84.9, which reads 84.

## A chip's list

A chip's list floats `space-xs` from its chip, beneath it where the window has room and above it where it does not. It is `surface` with a `line` edge, `radius-lg`, `shadow-transient`, padded `space-xs`, at least as wide as its chip and at most `size-menu`, on `stack-menu`. It fades and slides `space-xs` toward its place over `motion-quick` with `motion-ease-out`, and leaves the same way.

Opening a list moves keyboard focus to its chosen option; the arrow keys move among the options, Enter chooses one, and choosing or Escape closes the list and returns focus to its chip. The attach menu does the same, returning focus to attach.

- **An option** is `size-control` high, `radius-md`, padded `space-sm` at the sides, in `ui`: the option mark, its label in `ink`, and a status at its trailing edge in `ui-small` and `ink-faint`. An unavailable option reads `ink-faint`, keeps its status, and does not change under the pointer. The chosen option's mark is a dot at `size-option-mark` in `accent`; the others leave its place empty. An option is a control without an edge: under the pointer it takes `material-hover`, pressed `material-pressed`, and with keyboard focus an `ink` edge, so the focused option stays distinct from the one under the pointer.
- **An option with a description**, as a permission mode has (`claude-desktop/permission-menu-2026-11-24.txt`), is two lines tall, padded `space-xs` top and bottom, as a project's option is: the description sits under the label in `ui-small` and `ink-faint`, starting where the label starts. Its list is as wide as its longest description needs, up to `size-menu`, where longer text is cut with an ellipsis (`DESIGN.md`). An unavailable option's description reads `ink-faint` with its label.
- **An action at the end of a list**, such as Add project…, sits below a `line` hairline with `space-xs` above and below it, and leads with a plus icon at `size-icon-sm` in `ink-muted` in the mark's place. Its shortcut, Ctrl+O, sits at the trailing edge in `ui-small` and `ink-faint`.

## The project list

The project chip's list is the widest list, `size-menu` wide. Each project is an option two lines tall, padded `space-xs` top and bottom:

- **Line one**: the option mark, the project's name in `ui` and `ink`, then at the trailing edge its kind, Git or Folder, in `ui-small` and `ink-faint`. A project that is not trusted shows "Not trusted" after a shield icon at `size-icon-sm` in `caution`, in `ui-small` and `ink-muted`, before its kind.
- **Line two**: the folder's path in `ui-small` and `ink-faint`, under the name, cut with an ellipsis at its start so the folder's own name stays in view.

Add project… ends the list. **With no projects** the chip reads Choose a folder in `ink` with its folder icon, and opening it shows Add project… alone.

## The trust question

After a folder is picked, the trust question Ersa asks when a project is added opens above the composer's box, in the status line's place. It reads like the Claude Code CLI's folder-trust prompt, in Ersa's words, from top to bottom:

- **Title**: "Trust" and the folder's name, as a question, in `ui` at the heading weight.
- **Path**: the folder's full path in `mono` and `ink-muted`.
- **Safety check**: "Is this a project you created or one you trust, like your own code, a well-known open source project, or work from your team? If not, review what's in this folder first."
- **What agents can do**: "Agents will be able to read, edit, and run files here."
- **Answers**, in a row at its foot: Cancel, then Trust. Neither is primary: unlike other confirmations (CONTENT.md), trusting is never the default.

The text below the path is `ui-small` in `ink-muted`. The card is on `material-tile` with a `line` edge, `radius-lg`, padded `space-lg`. While it is open it holds the focal glow and the box shows its focus by its edge alone; the composer sends nothing (FLOW-NEW-SESSION-R42), send looks as it does with nothing to send, and the words can still be edited. It opens upward as the status line does, fading and growing over `motion-settle` with `motion-ease-out`, and leaves the same way. Keyboard focus moves to Cancel, so Enter cancels until the user moves to Trust; the arrow keys and Tab move between the answers, and Escape cancels. Once it is answered or canceled, and when the folder dialog is canceled, keyboard focus returns to the composer's words.

The question fits the window it opens in. The room above the box is what lies between the screen's top and the box. Where that room holds the title, the whole path, the safety check and the answers, the question opens over it and the box stays where it is; what the room can't hold of the text below the path scrolls inside the card, the safety check showing first. Where the room is shorter, the question takes its place in the column above the box instead: the box moves down, and the screen scrolls when the column is taller than it. There the card is as tall as the screen leaves it, though never shorter than its title, the first line of the path, the safety check and the answers; the path keeps what the height leaves it and scrolls inside itself, as the text below it does. So down to Ersa's smallest window, with a folder name that fits the title on one line, the title, the start of the path, the safety check and the answers show together.

Why the folder dialog couldn't open, or a picked folder couldn't be found or added, shows above the composer's box, in the status line's place, as a refused message does: the reason led by a glyph in `danger`, announced as an alert, until the user chooses another project or host from the chips, picks a folder again or sends a message (FLOW-NEW-SESSION-R44).

With reduced motion on, nothing here moves or fades: the glow, the status line, attachments and lists appear and go at once, and the detecting ring holds still at full tone.
